**Claims worth keeping** (all copied from the file)
- Thirty-six of Prof. Victor M. Ponce's hydrology and hydraulics calculators in one HTML file, in US or SI units; the libraries compute in SI and the switch changes only the display.
- "What is unique. Not the calculators." Ponce's portal at onlinecalc.sdsu.edu has had a web calculator for every method since 2004. What the file adds is the methods in one file with no server, a register of 57 defects each with the offending line quoted, and a 26-point comparison with the formulations on his own calculator pages. "The software is ordinary. The audit trail is new."
- 216 unit tests in 23 files pass, among them the Bulletin 17B frequency factor at skew 2 and T 100 (K = 3.605) and FAO-56 Examples 18 and 20.
- 26 formulation checks against Ponce's calculator pages and his culvert sample (yc = 4.037 ft, yn = 3.284 ft): worst relative difference 0.027 %.
- 41 routes rendered headless in US and SI, dark and light: 0 errors, 0 NaN, no horizontal overflow at 1280 or 375 px.
- Defect register: 57 defects fixed across v1.0 to v1.2 (22 high, 26 medium, 9 low); text audit: 32 corrections and 13 dead links replaced.
- Complexity 76.1 over 140 source files (Size 65.6, Structure 77.2, Test evidence 60.6, SWMM vocabulary 100.0; meter 1.7.0); the shipped .html alone 61.0.
- Human review: "Bob Dickinson directed the work and read the register; no hydrologist outside the project has reviewed the numbers."
- Netlify: https://ponce-hydrology-lab.netlify.app.

**Limits it states.** Lumped or one-dimensional and mostly steady: a teaching instrument, not a design code. The Ponce comparison is against his published formulations and one worked sample, not his calculators' live output, because "his servers refuse automated requests". The curve number method predicts zero runoff below Ia and is least accurate below 0.5 in of runoff. Spillway C does not vary with P/Hd; one culvert inlet type; Yang (1973) is a sand formula though the D50 slider reaches 3.94 in (100 mm; conversion ours); the Vedernikov module uses R = depth for every shape. Open: Thornthwaite is the unadjusted 12-hour-day form; FAO-56 Rn assumes sea level.
