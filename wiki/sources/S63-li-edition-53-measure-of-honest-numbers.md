**What it is.** Bob's newsletter edition announcing the app version of William James's book Rules for Responsible Modeling, with his recollections of James from 1978 and 1983. **Claims worth keeping**
- Bob first met Bill James in 1978, when James came to the University of Florida with Mark Robinson to teach a class on SWMM 2.
- The class of thirty ran SWMM 2 on a Boeing mainframe in Seattle through one "4k modem"; output came as continuous-feed printouts; input was on 80-column punch cards and a run could take 24 hours to come back.
- In that class James talked about data reliability, the difference between calibration and validation, and uncertainty.
- Bob was "one of the first people to use PCSWMM when it became available in 1983".
- James and Mark Robinson had an expert system that quizzed the user on the width parameter of SWMM 3.
- In 2005 James published Rules for Responsible Modeling: 303 pages, 17 chapters. The article names its subjects: reliability of input data, optimal complexity, sensitivity analysis, fuzzy logic, uncertainty disclosure and the engineer's ethical obligations.
- Overfitting, in the article's words: a model with enough adjustable parameters "can be made to fit any dataset", and the most dangerous model is the one that "succeeds too perfectly, and is believed".
- The app, at rulesforresponsiblemodeling.lovable.app, as of February 2026: 17 chapters; four guided pathways (student, 8 to 10 hours; practitioner review, 4 to 5 hours; ethics and uncertainty; all seventeen chapters); a diagnostic tool that starts from a symptom; a checklist generator giving a 40 item checklist by project phase (scoping, building, calibrating, reporting); templates for a scoping report, a calibration and validation log and an uncertainty disclosure.
- Bob's position: "The model is never the problem. SWMM 5 solves the Saint Venant equations correctly. InfoWorks ICM solves them correctly." The problem is the person who selects the data, chooses the complexity and interprets the output.
