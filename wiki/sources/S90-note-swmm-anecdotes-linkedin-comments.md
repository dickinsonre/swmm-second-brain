**What it is.** Recollections of SWMM history posted as public comments under one of Bob's LinkedIn posts in November 2026: four comments by Bob, and sentences from seven other people. Raw file: [raw/notes/swmm-anecdotes-linkedin-comments-2026-11.md](../../raw/notes/swmm-anecdotes-linkedin-comments-2026-11.md).

**How it was written.** Bob pasted the comment thread into a Claude session on 15 November 2026 and asked that it be kept as anecdotes of SWMM. Claude (Anthropic) typed the note from the paste. Bob's comments and the sentences that recall SWMM or modeling work are kept word for word under the names they were posted under. Comments that only congratulate, remarks about family or private life, job titles and reaction counts were left out, because the repository is public. The post itself was not in the paste. Bob has not yet read the note (Q64).

**What kind of evidence this is.** Recollections, written decades after the events, in a comment box. No dates are given beyond a decade. Where a claim here meets a primary document, the document outranks it.

**Claims worth keeping**
- Bob credits John Haestad with a year in Australia "to work on the XP-EXTRAN update" to the program he types as "XSWMM". He says Haestad was "the XP-EXTRAN reseller with Jack Cook", and that his career "would have been so different without that trip".
- Bob describes Haestad as inventive in the late 1980s with AutoCAD drainage models, and asks whether they were mostly the HEC series.
- Bob says Keith Hodsden Sr. was with Haestad's company. Keith Hodsden Sr. says he worked with Bob.
- E. R. R. Weaver started with SWMM on a Prime mainframe in the 1980s, after Bob had moved off punch cards, and says he "helped with SWMM3 revisions" while Bob was in graduate school at the University of Florida.
- Weaver credits Bob with adding "the double pipes we needed in the phosphate plant". He says a client he names as Gardinier would not have installed double 48 in HDPE pipes had the model not got "the backflows working through the ponds", and that the flow reversals were the harder problem.
- Weaver says he still has the reel tapes from those archives.
- Bob asks Weaver for SWMM 3 card decks with the card identifiers in columns 73 to 80, to build "a true runoff and transport 3".
- Bob hopes that with AI and SWMM6 he can "wrap up issues I remember from 1979".
- Hubert Jenny met Bob in Canberra in the early 1990s.
- Saša Tomić started his career on TU Delft's DUFLOW, which he calls the predecessor of SOBEK and D-HYDRO, and tested it in his graduate thesis. His remark on the two kinds of network: in EPANET there is "a very limited" ability to tell water what to do in a pressurized system, while "sewer rolls down the hill".
- Caleb Buahin thanks Bob for his "support and contributions" on SWMM6.
- Bob's LinkedIn headline on the day begins "SWMM since the 1977" and ends "99+ free SWMM apps".
