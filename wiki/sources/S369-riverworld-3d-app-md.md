**Claims worth keeping** (all copied from the file)
- A 3D model of Philip José Farmer's Riverworld at four zoom levels (globe, 60 miles of neighbouring reaches, a 10-mile valley at true scale with an eye-level bank view, and the polar sea), with three engineering checks on the novels' dimensions: surface budget, grailstone seats, and the fall the River needs.
- A 9-mile valley on an Earth-size planet has room for 21.9 million miles of River, so both of the books' lengths fit (10 million miles uses 46% of the surface, 20 million 91%). Grailstones at 700 seats seat 39% or 78% of the 36 billion people, and a 3 ft/s River at n 0.03 and 7,920 ft deep needs 5,308 ft of fall over 10 million miles; 61 of 61 checks pass.
- Length to seat everyone at 700 per stone: 25.7 million mi (more than fits).
- Fall for a 30 ft deep river, 10 million mi: 397 mi.
- Discharge 188.2 million cfs; power to lift it 5,308 ft: 113.3 billion hp (84,509 GW).
- Band-by-band sum of half circles of latitude reproduces 10 and 20 million mi within 0.1 %.

**Limits it states.** The planet view is a diagram: 24 switchbacks drawn against 632 or 1,263 called for. The east-west course is a guess; the sources give no map. The Reaches scene draws reaches straight and parallel at the calculated spacing. Plain and foothill widths are by eye. Mountain wall drawn at 20,000 ft from the builder's recollection, not confirmed by a source. Polar sea, gaps and tower have no sourced dimensions. Stones, huts and the boat are oversized, less so in the bank view; river bed not drawn to depth. Hydraulics: rectangular section, uniform flow, single n and V. Every book figure came through secondary sources; none checked against the novels. No person has reviewed the page.
