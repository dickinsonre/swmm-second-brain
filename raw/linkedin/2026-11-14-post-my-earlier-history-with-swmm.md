## The post, as pasted

ince I write about SWMM here so often, I wanted to share my earlier history with SWMM, my wife’s importance to my even being able to work on it, and a side note about how different it was to be a student in the 1970s. The image is from early 1981 in the days of SWMM3.

I learned about SWMM when I was 17. I met my wife when I was 18, we were married at 19, and I started working on SWMM when I was 22. What that leaves out is that I probably would never have worked on SWMM without her critical help when I was 20 and 21: she took a job at the library so I could stop working in restaurants. She was also taking a ten-credit-hour statistics class that involved a lot of late-night punch card work, so I learned programming from her, or at least got really good at managing punch cards. SWMM 2.5 was 20,000 punch cards, so it was a beast to manage. Bottom line, it is all a lucky accident (for me) to stll be able to help with [#SWMM6](https://www.linkedin.com/search/results/all/?keywords=%23swmm6&origin=HASH_TAG_FROM_FEED) now but also lucky to even be involved with SWMM in the first place.
