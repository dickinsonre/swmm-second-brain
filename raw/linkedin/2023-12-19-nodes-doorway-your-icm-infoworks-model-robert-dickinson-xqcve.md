# Nodes: the Doorway to your ICM InfoWorks Model

![Creative Manhole User](https://media.licdn.com/mediaD4E12AQFqKHPOWitFVQ "Creative Manhole User")

The different types of nodes used in hydraulic modeling in ICM InfoWorks, enhanced with festive Christmas emojis for a bit of holiday cheer:

> Manhole 🎄

Description: A node with shaft and chamber storage.

Function: Manholes are typically used in sewer systems to access the underground pipeline for inspection, cleaning, and maintenance. They provide vertical storage and can also be used to model flow diversion and junctions in the network.

![](https://media.licdn.com/dms/image/v2/D4E12AQGePo_g6mp5tQ/article-inline_image-shrink_1000_1488/article-inline_image-shrink_1000_1488/0/1703029085910?e=1795651200&v=beta&t=hq1buwXIMe8_jtvyN8qQKof5DDoSNfJD7bmcU8Iyjcs)

InfoWorks Data for a Manhole

> Storage 🎁

Description: Allows you to define your own storage shape using level/plan area pairs.

Function: Storage nodes are crucial for modeling detention facilities like basins or tanks. They help in managing excess runoff and controlling the release rate of stormwater into the drainage system.

> Break 🌟

Description: Can be used to model a change in gradient in a conduit. No storage.

Function: Break nodes are useful for representing changes in slope or elevation in a conduit, which can significantly affect the flow dynamics.

> Outfall 🎅

Description: Node where flow leaves the modeled system—no storage.

Function: Outfalls represent the points where the system discharges into a larger body of water or another system. They are essential for understanding the final disposition of the flow from the drainage system.

> Pond 🦌

Description: Similar to a Storage node, the Pond node also supports infiltration to the ground from the sides and the base of the pond.

Function: Ponds are used for retention and detention of stormwater, aiding in flood control and water quality improvement. They also allow for groundwater recharge through infiltration.

> Outfall 2D ❄️

Description: Outfall node from which flow is discharged to a 2D meshed area. Flow from the 2D area may also enter the 1D model via this node.

Function: This node bridges the 1D and 2D modeling aspects, allowing for more comprehensive floodplain mapping and analysis of complex flow patterns.

> Connect 2D 🎆

Description: A connection node that determines the way a conduit (2D) or linear drainage (2D) type of conduit exchanges flow at the upstream or downstream end vertex.

Function: These nodes are needed to add linear drainage features like swales or channels to a 2D surface flow model. This lets you see flow paths and speeds in more detail.

These nodes play a crucial role in the dynamic simulation of water flow in urban and natural environments, aiding in the effective planning, design, and management of water resources and flood control systems. 🌊📊🎉
