> A. My Reasons for being an Autodesk Subscriber

While I have access to equivalent free versions of software as an Autodesk employee, I choose to maintain a separate Autodesk subscription for several reasons:

1.  **Depth of Experience**: Having been involved in the development and evolution of modeling tools such as SWMM3, SWMM4, SWMM5, and XPSWMM, I recognize the value of experiencing software from a user's perspective. This perspective allows me to understand the challenges, benefits, and nuances that everyday users encounter better.
2.  **Staying Updated**: The AEC Collection, InfoDrainage, and ICM Standard offer features and updates that are easier to find as a paid Subscriber (may be wrong about this but I find it easier). Paying for the subscription ensures that I stay abreast of the latest advancements and experience firsthand the same tools that many industry professionals utilize.
3.  **Supporting the Industry**: My 50-year journey in modeling has always been about more than just personal growth. By subscribing to Autodesk software, I'm supporting the continuous development and refinement of tools that benefit the entire industry. It's my way of giving back and ensuring that these tools continue to serve at their best.
4.  **Unique Relationship with Autodesk**: My dual connection with Autodesk—both as an employee and as a subscriber—provides me with a comprehensive view of the software landscape. This all-encompassing perspective is invaluable in my endeavors to assist others in modeling.
5.  **Trusted Reseller Partnership**: My relationship with CADD Microsystems ensures that my subscription journey is seamless. Their expertise and unwavering support make maintaining the subscription worthwhile.
6.  **Dual Channels for Contribution**: Being a subscriber to Autodesk software offers me a unique dual avenue for engagement. I can actively contribute ideas and solutions to the forums, enriching the community's knowledge pool. Furthermore, I have the privilege of helping shape the software's future through User Voice. The [User Voice platform](https://innovyzefeedback.autodesk.com/ideas) stands as a pivotal channel where user's can suggest enhancements and innovations. My subscription ensures that my voice, steeped in decades of experience, resonates with both developers and the user community.

In essence, my decision to subscribe is deeply rooted in a commitment to the industry, a thirst for continuous learning, and a proactive approach to influence and enhance the user experience of the software.

> C. How Does the Subscription Work for Me?

Simply put, I utilize my Gmail account as the primary subscriber to Autodesk's ICM Standard, InfoDrainage Standard, and the AEC Collection. Additionally, I take advantage of Flex, which allows me to seamlessly tap into other Autodesk tools like Fusion 360 or Maya on an as-needed basis. For the higher-tier offerings, such as ICM and InfoDrainage Ultimate, I use my Autodesk email as the account owner. For legacy software and those soon maturing, like InfoSWMM and InfoSewer, I rely on the robust Thales licensing system from Innovyze.

A feature I genuinely appreciate is the ability to effortlessly switch between my numerous PCs—achieved by simply logging into my Autodesk account. This adaptability speaks to Autodesk's dedication to user convenience. To stay updated, I employ Autodesk Access, ensuring I'm always in sync with the latest patches and developments. It's worth highlighting that my subscription journey is greatly enhanced by CADD Microsystems, an exceptional Autodesk reseller who consistently assists me with my subscription needs

> D. Legacy Innovyze Software (A list from my PC)

Since joining Innovyze in 2007, I've had the privilege of amassing a diverse portfolio of both legacy and current software, primarily centered around the EPASWMM engine. My personal collection features esteemed tools like XPSWMM, H2OMap Sewer, H2OCalc, InfoSewer (which interestingly has a few SWMM components), InfoSewer SA, H2OMap SWMM, InfoSWMM SA, InfoSWMM, H2OMap Water, InfoWater, InfoWater SA, InfoWater Pro, InfoWorks CS, InfoWorks RS, and 20 versions of InfoWorks ICM variations. Notably, ICM InfoWorks networks integrate at least ten hydrology and water quality components from SWMM, showcasing the software's comprehensive capabilities.

At present, we're actively supporting and selling InfoSWMM and InfoSewer. However, with ESRI's impending phase-out of Arc Map support, this dynamic will evolve. To ensure you remain at the cutting edge, we've designed an I2S upgrade plan tailored for you, transitioning to ICM Ultimate. This advanced solution marries both SWMM and InfoWorks networks in ICM.

Integration remains our hallmark. For InfoSWMM aficionados, a direct import to ICM is at your fingertips. InfoSewer enthusiasts can leverage scripts on our GitHub for a smooth transition to ICM. And for XPSWMM users, a direct import pathway is established to ICM. Our commitment remains unwavering: ensuring any model you've crafted in the past imports seamlessly with the current versions of InfoWorks, preserving the integrity of your work across eras.

> E. Legacy models to Current and Future models have Import Tools in Depth

Having Import tools in depth underscores Autodesk Innovyze's commitment to ensuring seamless transitions across its software generations. At its core, it signifies that Autodesk Innovyze provides not just direct import capabilities for models from legacy products, but also offers an extensive suite of advanced import tools.

Thanks to the inherent flexibility of ICM, users benefit from a myriad of customization options. They can employ SQL and Ruby Scripts to fine-tune the imported model data, ensuring precision and alignment with project needs. Moreover, with the Open Data Import Centre at their disposal, users have access to dozens of methods to import data, further enhancing the adaptability and versatility of the platform. This comprehensive approach ensures that every model, regardless of its origin, can be integrated and optimized using Autodesk Innovyze tools.

> F. Personal Reasons for being a Subscriber

Over the course of my illustrious journey, a multitude of personal reasons have shaped my perspective, and they coalesce into eight pivotal categories:

1.  **Half a Century with SWMM**: This year marks the golden jubilee of my tryst with SWMM, a journey that began at the age of 17 during an Introduction to Environmental Engineering class at the University of Florida. From the onset, it seamlessly intertwined my three passions: water, the environment, and the art of engineering.
2.  **Decades with My Anchor**: Celebrating the 49th year since I met my wife, her unwavering support has been monumental. Her multifaceted roles as an exceptional wife, diligent grad student, adept technical worker, and a nurturing mother have been instrumental, enabling me to dedicate extended hours to my craft over four decades.
3.  **The Crucible of Health**: My encounter with a complex form of cancer in 2021 and 2022 [detailed here](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/uncommon-synergy-link-node-hydraulic-modeling-nhl-t-cell-dickinson) accentuated my appreciation for life and deepened my gratitude for my profession as an engineer.
4.  **Resilience Personified**: 2022 saw my wife grappling with a benign brain tumor surgery, which resulted in a stroke affecting her right side. This adversity underscored the invaluable support she provided over the years. As she navigates her recovery, I've grown to further cherish the intricacies of human cognition and the marvel of learning.
5.  **An Autodesk Epoch**: Autodesk resonates deeply with me. My engineering journey paralleled AutoCAD's inception, and my programming endeavors often intersected with DWF and DWG files. To me, Autodesk isn't merely a name; it's a realm of endless possibilities.
6.  **Colleagues, the Pillars**: My coworkers, some of whom I've collaborated with for an incredible 17 years, have been my stalwarts. Their unwavering support, especially during the tumultuous phases of 2021 onwards, has been invaluable, helping me navigate personal health challenges and my wife's care. Furthermore, I stand in awe of their technical wizardry, video prowess, writing aptitude, and their genuine care for customers.
7.  **Legacy of Learning**: My engineer children and grandchildren, potential engineers in their own right, have consistently amazed me with their intellect and creativity from a tender age. Their innovative approach has been a continuous source of inspiration and learning.
8.  **Mentors & Collaborators**: Over five decades, I've been privileged to work alongside numerous SWMM experts, with special mention to Wayne Huber and Lew Rossman, who have been instrumental in my growth.

You might ponder the connection between these experiences and my role as a subscriber. To me, they're intrinsically linked. My subscription embodies my commitment to the software's future, transcending my identity as an employee. It's a testament to my dedication to our legacy programs and converting and saving old models. Through this, I aim to empower you to be superior modelers, employing the software just as you do, underscoring my shared journey in this domain.
