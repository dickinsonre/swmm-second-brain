# My SWMM Enablement Newsletter Plans for Nov/Dec 2026

![My SWMM Enablement Newsletter Plans for Nov/Dec 2026](https://media.licdn.com/mediaD4E12AQFOuSvJKwd2zQ "My SWMM Enablement Newsletter Plans for Nov/Dec 2026")

I should lead each edition with one theme and keep all six visible in a short footer.

Here’s why one theme should lead:

- **One hook.** A LinkedIn newsletter gets one subject line and one opening paragraph. Six themes in one edition become a table of contents, and people skim tables of contents.

- **Your time.** Covering all six every week means writing six things a week. One lead means one solid piece, and that matches one [swmm5.org](http://swmm5.org) page and one theme a week.

- **Depth.** “Inside SWMM5” or the SWMM1 history needs room for numbers, a GIF and the code line that matters. A paragraph each doesn’t give you that.

- **Rotation.** With six themes, each one leads every six weeks. Readers who care about ICM vs SWMM6 learn when it comes around.

The footer stays the same in every edition. It’s six lines, one per theme, each linking to that theme’s page on [swmm5.org](http://swmm5.org). Add “New this week” beside any theme that got a new app or post. Readers still see all six every time, and the footer sends traffic to the pages that will still be findable in 2030. I am using 2030 as a drop dead date since i will be 75. Not to say that will not continue but time is finite and I wanted a deadline.

A layout for each edition:

1.  **The idea.** One finding with a number, in two or three sentences.

2.  **The app.** A GIF, the Netlify link and what to try first.

3.  **What I found.** The nitty-gritty, with the one code line or equation behind it.

4.  **What to do Monday.** One thing a modeler can change in their own model.

5.  **Elsewhere.** Optional: one or two lines on another theme with something new.

6.  **The six-theme footer**, plus the line disclosing your AI use.

### A few more thoughts before the first edition

**Have two editions ready before I send the first.** A small backlog is what keeps a schedule honest. If a week gets away from me, the next edition is already written.

**Be realistic about Nov/Dec.** With Thanksgiving and the holidays, these two months hold maybe four good editions, not eight thin ones. A predictable rhythm matters more than a fast one, so every other week is fine if readers know when to expect it.

**Lead the first edition with what is already finished.** The theme with the most material already on [swmm5.org](http://swmm5.org) should go first. That way the first edition is mostly editing, not writing from scratch.

**Don’t rename pages that are already indexed.** Renaming the [swmm5.org](http://swmm5.org) topic pages would break links that search engines and readers already use, which works against the 2030 goal. Six short theme pages that point into the existing topic pages are cheaper and safer.

**Let the footer be the experiment.** After one full rotation of six themes, I can see which theme pages got clicks from the newsletter. That tells me what to lead with in 2027, without guessing.

**End each edition with one question.** Modelers love to say where SWMM or ICM surprised them. Those comments are the raw material for future editions.

**Draft the AI line once and reuse it.** Something like: “Written by me, with AI help for drafting, code and figures. The engineering judgment, and any mistakes, are mine.”

**The newsletter is the delivery truck;** [**swmm5.org**](http://swmm5.org) **is the warehouse.** If every edition leaves behind a permanent page, a working app and a model file, the work keeps answering questions long after 2030.
