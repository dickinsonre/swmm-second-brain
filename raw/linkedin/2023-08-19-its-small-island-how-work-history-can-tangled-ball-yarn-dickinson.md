# It's A Small Island - How A Work History Can be a Tangled Ball of Yarn if the Yarn is composed of SWMM Versions

![EPASWMM Download Page](https://media.licdn.com/mediaD4E12AQHY0Vk2Y7f2JA "EPASWMM Download Page")

Now, 50 years is a long time, and time does not seem to go faster as you get older, which is good. If you look at this timeline, there are multiple instances where an association comes into play and then never goes away, but instead becomes a looser association. I've been involved with Autodesk three times in 40 years, and have had scattered interactions with DWF and DXF files. CDM comes into play six times, the USEPA three major times, Wayne Huber and Lew Rossman dozens of times, Wallingford three major times in 35 years, UF for 20 years, and XP SWMM or XP Software three times. Additionally, I've been involved with ESRI for 20 years, Innovyze or MWH Soft for 20 years. CAiCE and Road Design provided a strong view of AutoCAD for a few years. The common thread in this ball of yarn is SWMM

- **1971**: Wayne Huber, along with his team including Jim Heaney and at least 20 graduate students over 20 years and various SWMM versions, and WRE, co-developed SWMM1 while at the University of Florida (UF). SWMM was developed for the USEPA as a tool for urban stormwater runoff analysis and planning.
- **1973**: Bob Dickinson attended a survey class which included SWMM at UF at age 17. The class introduced him to the intricacies of stormwater management and modeling.
- **1975**: CDM bought WRE. WRE had co-developed SWMM1 and later Extran in 1975. SWMM2 was released.
- **1981**: SWMM3 was released.
- **1980s**: Wayne Huber and Bob Dickinson co-authored SWMM4 in 1988. This version introduced significant advancements in the modeling of stormwater systems.
- **1987**: Wallingford visited UF for two weeks, meeting with Wayne Huber. Bob Dickinson got to see some of the inner workings of the Wallingford pre-HydroWorks software, which was important for SWMM4 development.
- **1990**: Lew Rossman of the USEPA began developing EPANET, a software application for modeling drinking water distribution systems.
- **1992**: Bob Dickinson moved to Australia with his family to develop XPSWMM, which merged SWMM4 and Extran 4 into one Engine and GUI, providing users with an integrated modeling experience.
- **1993-1998**: Bob Dickinson served as EVP of XP Software, contributing to the development and success of XPSWMM.
- **1998**: CAiCE Software, which had road design software and VBA for AutoCAD, merged with USA XP Software, bringing together their complementary technologies.
- **1998-2000**: Bob Dickinson worked at CAiCE Software, where he developed Visual SWMM and Visual Hydro, expanding the software's capabilities for hydrologic and hydraulic modeling.
- **1999-2009**: InfoWorks CS was developed, incorporating some SWMM4 components. This software allowed users to model complex stormwater systems with greater ease and accuracy.
- **2001**: CAiCE Software was bought by Autodesk, which was founded in 1982.
- **2001-2005**: Lew Rossman of the USEPA developed SWMM5 in a CRADA with CDM. Bob Dickinson, working at CDM from 2000 to 2008, and Wayne Huber, now at OSU, were co-developers. This version further advanced the modeling of stormwater systems.
- **2002**: MWH Soft developed InfoWater based on EPANET, providing users with a powerful tool for modeling drinking water distribution systems.
- **2005**: InfoSWMM was developed by MWH Soft based on SWMM5, offering an integrated modeling environment for stormwater systems.
- **2008-2021**: Bob Dickinson worked at Innovyze as Product Sector Leader for InfoSWMM and InfoSewer, contributing to the development and success of these software products.
- **2009**: InfoWorks CS evolved into InfoWorks ICM, incorporating SWMM4 and SWMM5 components. MWH Soft bought Wallingford Software, bringing together their complementary technologies.
- **2010**: MWH Soft and Wallingford Software merged to become Innovyze, creating a powerhouse in water modeling software.
- **2016**: Stantec bought Innovyze and its parent company MWH.
- **2017**: Stantec sold Innovyze to venture capital firm EQT, allowing Innovyze to focus on expanding its software offerings.
- **2020**: InfoWater Pro was developed, based on EPANET. This software provided users with an advanced modeling environment for drinking water distribution systems.
- **2021**: Autodesk bought Innovyze. InfoWorks ICM now includes ICM SWMM, which uses the SWMM5 Engine, and InfoDrainage, which has a SWMM5 engine at its core. ICM InfoWorks is now a subscription product from Autodesk and has two versions: Standard and Ultimate.

Throughout this timeline, various software programs were developed wholly or partially based on SWMM, including XPSWMM, InfoWorks CS, InfoWorks ICM, and InfoSWMM (2005). ICM now has SWMM4 and SWMM5 components such as RDII, infiltration, water quality, hydrology, snowmelt, and SuDS. As part of Autodesk, the software can interact with the AEC (Architecture, Engineering, and Construction) collection of software, which includes tools for architectural design, civil infrastructure, and construction such as AutoCAD, Revit, Civil 3D, InfraWorks, and Forma.

Finally SWMM Versions and the AEC Collection along with the Flagship products from Innovyze now sold as Subscriptions in Autodesk so Autodesk ICM InfoWorks Ultimate or Standard for example.
