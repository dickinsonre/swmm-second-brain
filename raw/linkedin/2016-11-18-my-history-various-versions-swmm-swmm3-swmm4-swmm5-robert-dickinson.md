# My History with Various Versions of SWMM: SWMM3, SWMM4, XP-SWMM, SWMM5, SWMM5+, SWMM Networks in ICM and InfoSWMM - 2023 Update

![](https://media.licdn.com/mediaC5112AQHI8TOugdPmLQ)

**Subject:**  **My History with Various Versions of SWMM: SWMM 2.5, SWMM3, SWMM4, SWMM5, SWMM5+, XPSWMM, Visual SWMM and InfoSWMM along with SWMM5 Features in InfoWorks ICM InfoWorks and SWMM Networks and now Autodesk Innoyze InfoDrainage**

> I first discovered SWMM when I was just a 17-year-old senior in high school, reading a brochure from the University of Florida.

The idea of water resources and the description of SWMM seemed like worthy career goals at the time, and I have been incredibly fortunate to have had the opportunity to work on and develop many SWMM-related products throughout my career.

One of my proudest achievements is that SWMM 3, SWMM 4, and SWMM 5 are all public domain and open-source software. The code is open and available for anyone to view, and users can add their features and internal tests. This has allowed for greater collaboration and innovation in the field, and I am proud to have been a part of it.  

[https://www.epa.gov/water-research/storm-water-management-model-swmm](https://www.epa.gov/water-research/storm-water-management-model-swmm)[http://www.ncimm.org/](http://www.ncimm.org/)

> You can also customize the input and output of SWMM 5 if you want to but most importantly you have direct access to the source code so you can verify the computational algorithms.

InfoSWMM is an Arc GIS extension that works in Arc Map, but we at Innovyze are proud that we have excellent import and export features to SWMM 5 from Arc Map and use the SWMM 5 C code as our engine solution.  You can look at the internal workings of the InfoSWMM engine by downloading the current SWMM 5 C code from the EPA website [http://www.epa.gov/nrmrl/wswrd/wq/models/swmm/](http://www.epa.gov/nrmrl/wswrd/wq/models/swmm/).  

> **InfoSWMM** also has all of the **Arc GIS** programming tools (Arc Toolbox), Python programming, editing and drawing features and Table of Contents attribute features to make a better model but that model will still use the open source SWMM 5 engine inside of **InfoSWMM**.   

When you use InfoSWMM you are using the SWMM 5 engine but with many extra input and output, features to help prepare the data for the current SWMM 5 engine and analyze the SWMM 5 engine output in maps, tables, and graphs.

> **My life in the CSD Division of CDM (now CDM Smith in 2003)**

Bob Dickinson, a consultant at CDM CSD Consulting Division in 2003, has a proven track record of delivering exceptional results to his clients. His specialties lie in Marketing Success and Client Service & Project Delivery. He excels at adding unique, customized features to the Stormwater Management Model (SWMM) that correspond to each client's needs.

In Marketing Success, Bob has demonstrated his ability to tailor the SWMM to specific client requirements. For Miami/Dade, he added population/pump curves to the model, which allowed the client to match SCADA data with pump data. For Winnipeg, he added flow regulators, which made it possible to model the system effectively.

Bob's Client Service & Project Delivery skills are exemplified in his work on the ALCOSAN modeling project. He was able to add specialized snowmelt, rainfall, temperature, runoff, statistical, diurnal flow patterns, and hydraulic features that enabled successful modeling of the Pittsburgh separate and combined sewer systems. In a Water Quality Study of Nine Mile Run in Pittsburgh, he developed an EMC input for SWMM and an ACCESS interface between SWMM and ArcView to predict watershed and CSO loadings for 50 years. Bob also offers training and white papers to help clients understand the computer models they pay for and offers translation services for clients with older Stormwater models. He can also program customized interfaces or applications for clients using Fortran, VB, or C++ for water quality, drainage, road design, 3D visualization, statistical analysis, and stormwater and sanitary hydraulic analysis.

Bob has an impressive educational background, with a M.E. in Environmental Engineering Sciences and a B.E. in Environmental Engineering Services, both from the University of Florida, in 1985 and 1979, respectively. He is also a developer of SWMM Versions 3 and 4 at UF, XP-SWMM at XP Software, Visual Hydro, and Visual Culvert at CAiCE Software.

Since joining CDM in late 2000, Bob has helped the firm advance with the SWMM 5 upgrade initiative. He has also bridged the gap between SWMM vendors and the public domain engine of SWMM by enabling CDM to become a link between the various SWMM vendors. His achievements and innovations in the field of SWMM have earned him a reputation as a thought leader and expert in the industry.
